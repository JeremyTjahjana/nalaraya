# Epidermis content notes

The activity adapts wet-mount preparation and light-microscope observation, not osmosis. Sources consulted:

- CCEA, Practical Skills 1.1: temporary slide preparation and use of a light microscope: https://ccea.org.uk/downloads/docs/Support/Practical%20Manual/2019/Practical%20Manual%20Unit%203%3A%20Practical%20Skills%201.1%20Carry%20out%20practical%20work%20to%20make%20a%20temporary%20slide%20and%20use%20a%20light%20microscope%20Guide.pdf
- AQA / Hodder GCSE Biology sample, onion epidermis with forceps, unfolded tissue and iodine: https://filestore.aqa.org.uk/textbooks/sample/gcse-biology/AQA-8461-HODDER-SAMPLE.PDF
- Walter Dioni, direct microscopy observations of inner onion epidermis, preparation and iodine staining: https://www.microscopy-uk.org.uk/mag/artapr11/wd-onion3.html

The diagram is an authored teaching illustration. Cell wall, central vacuolar region and peripheral nucleus are intentionally distinguishable; this is not a calibrated optical simulation or a micrograph. Lugol increases contrast but does not guarantee equally clear nuclei in real preparations. Vacuoles are represented as large interior regions, without claiming that their membranes are resolved. Bulb epidermal cells generally do not show chloroplasts.

No cutting tools are introduced: the onion material is treated as teacher-prepared. Water metadata and geometry reuse the chemistry wash bottle, with contextual biology copy.
